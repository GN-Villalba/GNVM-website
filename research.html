Research

PhD Research

  • Raising the Floor: Minimum Quality Standards and the Training of Future Teachers in Colombia. View PhD Poster PhD chapter evaluating the effects of mandatory quality accreditation of teacher training programmes in Colombia on student outcomes and higher-education provision, using linked administrative data and a staggered difference-in-differences and event-study design. Presented at the Royal Economic Society Annual Conference 2026.
  • What Students Want and Where They End Up: Aspirations, Local Options and Undermatch in Colombia. PhD chapter studying educational mismatch at the programme level in Colombia. It links administrative records of secondary-school achievement and university enrolment with aspirations students reported before knowing their exam results, and with local educational supply, to compare the opportunities students aimed for with those they ultimately obtained, and how these differ by socioeconomic background and gender. Presented at the 2nd Junior Workshop on Economics of Latin America (Freie Universität Berlin, 2026) and the Sussex PGR Workshop in Economics (2026); accepted at the Scottish Economic Society Annual Conference 2027.

Other Working Papers

  • Gender Pay Gaps in a Middle-Income Country: Household Roles and the Motherhood Penalty.
    Working paper derived from MSc research.
    View Draft on OSF This paper examines gender wage inequalities across the earnings distribution in Paraguay, with a focus on household roles and the motherhood penalty, using nationally representative household survey data.
  • Women-Only Admission Tracks in Japanese Universities: Effects, Selectivity and Limits.
    with Tomoto Masuda. Work in progress. Studies the effects of women-only admission tracks on who is admitted to Japanese universities, and the selectivity and limits of these policies.
  • Pro Rata, Not Parity: The Allocation of Women's Quotas in Japanese STEM.
    with Tomoto Masuda. Work in progress. A theoretical analysis of how women's admission quotas are allocated across STEM programmes in Japanese universities.
